## 1. Purpose and non-negotiable interpretation

This register separates **documented historical features**, **unconfirmed implementation details**, and **FreeIndustry design choices**. Its purpose is to capture the feel and strategic lessons of the 1996 manufacturing-business simulation without passing off an invented model as Tsunami Media's original code or copying its protected expression.

The reference game's central loop, as described in multiple independent accounts, is: **choose a market and factory → choose products → finance the enterprise → arrange industrial equipment, storage, offices and labor → delegate purchasing and sales → run an operating interval → watch staff, material flows and manufacturing → inspect results → revise the business**. The exceptional quality worth preserving is the connection between the corporate spreadsheet and a _visible, spatial factory staffed by imperfect people_. The original combined manufacturing, employee personalities, supply and demand, banking, research and competitors; its systems were not necessarily economically accurate or user-friendly. [GF], [OG], [MG], [GS], [MGR]

**Important distinction:** "The original had this feature" does **not** mean "we know the original algorithm." A historical description or screenshot can establish a screen, choice, or reported effect while leaving timing, probabilities, thresholds, and data structures unknown. Do not derive exact formulas from anecdotes.

**Confidence is independent of status.** `HIGH` means multiple mutually supporting sources or an unusually specific original-era screenshot; `MEDIUM` means credible, specific secondary testimony; `LOW` means one anecdote, approximate description, uncertainty, or an untested negative assertion. No source is treated as an executable specification. Screenshot evidence is an observation of a _visible state_, not proof of the internal algorithms. A published player review is firsthand _testimony_ but not a reproducible captured session.

## 2. High-impact corrections to the seed ledger

1. **Operating turns were reportedly _not_ interruptible.** GF §5.3 describes choosing a duration, watching a three-quarter operating view, and returning to the top-down editable view at the end. MGR reports a one-day minimum and that equipment placement/task assignments were locked while running. FreeIndustry's pause, replan and targeted advance are `EXTENDED` improvements, not original fidelity. [GF §5.3], [MGR]
2. **The original factory was not quite an empty room.** GF §5.1 describes existing storage zones, employee entry, freight doors and a staff-room group with break, meeting and toilet areas. FreeIndustry's desired _genuinely empty industrial space_—player purchases/places all initial stations and amenities—is `EXTENDED`. [GF §5.1]
3. **Do not claim original multi-facility expansion.** One detailed MGR review says the chosen city/building remains fixed in a save. The original supports deepening/diversifying operations _within_ a factory; FreeIndustry's multiple plants, relocation and networked logistics are additional scope until contrary firsthand evidence appears. [MGR, 2005 review]
4. **Original research had a very distinctive puzzle.** Researchers used a dedicated production-class machine, selected a target technology/product and guessed material/component combinations. Wrong combinations could fail partway through, returning partial hints; a completed project could activate the machine for production. Treat this distinctive _interaction pattern_ as documented, but not the underlying research function. [GF §6.1], [RM]
5. **Material handling was spatial and constrained.** Machine ports, operators, safety zones, storage-zone product limitations, hand carts, forklifts, conveyor endpoints and storage bins were not merely decorative. The stated restrictions were unusually specific. [GF §§4.2.1, 5.1, 5.2.3, 6.3]
6. **Financial choices and optional realism modes are documented.** Funding routes included inheritance, debt and outside capital; reported switches included `Free Economy`, `Auto Paperwork`, `Robo Workers`, `Editable Personalities`, and `Postal Mode`. Replicate their _design purposes_ only if desired; recreate labels/UI and simulation effects independently. [GF §3.1], [MS]
7. **The product chain included parts sold to other manufacturers.** This matters more than the exact old catalog: it supports a real input/output graph rather than isolated factories producing directly for consumers. [GF §§3, 6.3], [MGR]
8. **A user-facing encyclopedia/ledger must be reproducible.** No row is upgraded to `VERIFIED_ORIGINAL` merely because a walkthrough has a precise number (e.g., transport capacity, per-spy fee, employee attribute count). Those values are documented claims, not tested original invariants.

## 4. What the historical sources actually teach us about the game's design

This section is a **paraphrased systems analysis**, not a rewrite of the manuals or walkthrough. It elaborates the ledger rows into mechanisms that a designer or coding agent can reason about. Specific original numeric values mentioned below are marked as **reported** or **visible example**, never adopted as required FreeIndustry settings.

### 4.1 Game setup was a business-location problem, not just a terrain picker

The player began at a U.S. map and drilled down into one city's industrial area. The choice connected at least four categories of information:

- **Cost of premises:** vacant buildings differed by area and rental charge; the walk-through says centrally located lots were usually more expensive.
- **Labor economics:** city descriptions exposed job-market wage averages and some form of workforce experience/quality information.
- **Input availability:** not every city had the same suppliers or access to upstream materials.
- **Demand and industry structure:** some occupied buildings represented companies that manufactured or consumed the selected products, giving local supplier/buyer and competitor clues.

A player's early strategic decision was therefore _where to locate_ and _which component/product to manufacture first_. Buildings were not generic blank investments interchangeable with all other property. In FreeIndustry the player should make an intelligible decision from rents, worker supply, logistical access, existing companies and potential customers—not from a magic best-city score.

**Scale qualifiers:** RM describes **50 selectable U.S. cities** and more than **100 machinery types**; MG's promotional description says **up to 100 products**. Those are approximate, source-dependent catalog claims. Do not treat them as exact version-neutral counts or replicate them as fixed requirements. [RM intro], [MG]

### 4.2 Original capital had a payoff, control and risk trade-off

GF describes three initial sources of capital: personal/inherited resources, loans, and outside investors. More money could buy a bigger production setup but put debt repayment or company ownership at risk. GS independently reports startup funding choices spanning about **$400,000–$1,000,000** and greater obligations for the larger choices. An original-game screenshot reproduced at OA visibly shows a **$400,000** initial deposit and a particular rent debit; it is _one state_, not evidence all players start with the same funds or rent.

**FreeIndustry design takeaways:**

1. Starting money must be a **real balance**. Buying a machine, paying rent or buying stock changes it once, through a ledger entry.
2. Debt is not just an immediate cash injection. It must have disbursement, payment obligations, rates, maturity and failure consequences.
3. Equity is not merely a free cash option. Ownership percentage, expected return and investor controls should matter when this mode is enabled.
4. Funding presets should never introduce hidden money, waived obligations, or ghost vendors.

The system should support starting from a smaller, harder business than the original if that's more satisfying, but make the difference explicit.

### 4.3 The empty-floor illusion concealed genuine operational infrastructure

GF's factory description contains a specific spatial grammar:

- One employee entry/exit.
- Distinct incoming and outgoing freight access.
- Pre-existing general storage area(s).
- Employee support area containing meeting, break and restrooms.
- Buildable floor for machines, offices, smoke/safety areas and material-handling equipment.

**Do not confuse this with FreeIndustry's design decision.** The proposed FreeIndustry baseline is a vacant factory shell: doors and building services may exist because they're part of leased real estate, but machinery, production staff, office desks, movable storage devices, forklifts and supplies do not magically come with the player company. If a playable empty shell needs compliant restrooms or exit corridors, those may belong to the property, but distinguish them from _company-purchased_ amenities.

### 4.4 Machine behavior depends on more than its on/off setting

The walkthrough's descriptions imply a physical, role-based workstation model rather than a single progress bar detached from space. A manufacturing station can need:

- One or more _specific_ input materials.
- Access to the material input side and a place to deposit materials.
- Assigned people at machine control/handling positions.
- Service access for repairs and maintenance staff.
- Free output capacity or a conveyor/storage destination.
- Reasonable logistics path from inventory to machine and from machine to finished stock.
- Functional condition (not broken/in fire) and suitable safety area.

GF's machine-nonproduction FAQ calls out missing materials, missing people, full storage or malfunction. It does **not** identify the exact original job-dispatch algorithm, so FreeIndustry should define and test its own.

**Reference-quality end-to-end example, deliberately using new fictional product names:**

`Inbound truck delivers 120 units of material A` → `Receiving operator unloads 120` → `Stored lot increases by 120` → `Worker reserves 24 for line 1` → `Worker transports physically` → `Machine starts job only when required labor and materials are present` → `Job consumes 24 input units` → `Job produces a countable output lot` → `Operator moves pallet to storage` → `Sales office books an order` → `Freight dispatch removes the correct units` → `Receivable and/or bank entry posted when applicable`.

If the animation shows a worker doing an action but the simulation records no corresponding task or transaction, something is wrong. Conversely, no production or shipping quantity may appear from a disconnected graphical animation alone.

### 4.5 Layout should produce meaningful operational trade-offs

Reference mechanisms include:

- **Storage zones** limited to one stock type at a time (GF).
- **Hand carts/forklifts** for larger loads; operators still sometimes choose direct carrying (GF).
- **Conveyor belts** for direct downstream supply or transfer into buffers (GF).
- **Storage bins** used as collection/distribution points and surplus buffers (GF).
- **Safety zones** at certain machine inputs (GF).
- **Office quality and noise separation** affecting administrative performance/stress (GF/OG).
- **Service access** preventing machines or belts from blocking people and equipment (GF).

The original apparently disallowed some connections, rotations, access crossings and bin combinations. These are valuable **design prompts**, not sacred constraints. In FreeIndustry, the player should be told _why_ a placement is invalid: `machine service port blocked`, `insufficient turning clearance`, `no output path`, `fire exit obstructed`, `truck dock cannot reach zone`, etc. Player intuition should generally outweigh obscure undocumented grid exceptions.

### 4.7 Staff acted as agents, not just employee-count multipliers

The historical sources repeatedly distinguish Free Enterprise from many earlier tycoons by the _individual_ workforce. Applications expose job-specific aptitude and requested pay, and people can work, socialize, relax, smoke, become stressed, perform below expectations and communicate with the president. The personnel window shows recent actions and a stress graph. Office, machine and managerial workers perform different duties. Some relevant behaviors are only anecdotal; there is no recovered general-purpose original AI.

**FreeIndustry should make labor satisfy five kinds of constraints**:

1. **Eligibility:** training, job permissions, assigned equipment and station location.
2. **Availability:** clock time, shifts, attendance, breaks, health/safety state and task conflicts.
3. **Capacity:** how much work can this person complete under current task difficulty and environmental conditions?
4. **Quality:** scrap risk, bookkeeping mistakes, procurement choices, client service and rework.
5. **Preference and retention:** compensation, fatigue, management treatment, satisfaction and other job offers.

On-screen agents are **presentations of authoritative tasks**: a `LoadMachine` task should show the worker traveling, handling stock and returning to work. A person cannot be booked on two full-capacity physical tasks simultaneously.

**Fairness note:** 1996-period source descriptions associate personality, smoking/substance use and breakdown in simplistic ways. There is no reason to inherit potentially stigmatizing assumptions. Preserve meaningful individual differences and workplace needs; model unsafe behavior only where grounded in specific observable actions and game balance.

### 4.8 Office labor mattered because of a real administration bottleneck

Both original-game realism options and hiring advice indicate that the business did not operate fully on its own unless the player selected an automatic-paperwork mode. GF describes purchaser, salesperson, accountant, president, maintenance and operator as a practical core team; RM adds that administrative roles such as a clerk, controller and directors matter in certain configurations.

Build **work queues and accountable outcomes**, not hidden scalar boosts:

The **exact work queues above are FreeIndustry proposals**, not alleged internals of Free Enterprise. Their requirement is to ensure office staffing has material, testable operational consequences.

### 4.9 The operating turn was a simulation-and-observation sequence

The historical UI is best understood as two phases:

1. **Planning phase:** select/rotate/place equipment, schedule people, set price/purchasing rules, compare reports, plan the next interval.
2. **Operating phase:** click `Start Turn`, choose elapsed time, observe a three-quarter view, see people and freight move, receive notices, then return to planning.

GF warns that a player cannot prematurely stop the chosen period. MGR reports that the player cannot reassign jobs or install machinery while it runs and complains about speed. GF describes an option that slows display during significant events.

FreeIndustry replaces those usability constraints while preserving the **causal excitement of watching the enterprise function**:

- Pause and resume without changing game state other than time progression.
- Speed changes control presentation/calendar progression, not production economics.
- Production scheduling remains stable through frame-rate changes.
- `Advance until tomorrow's opening`, `until shipment`, `until job finishes`, `until first exception` are convenience controls, not new economic sources.
- Changes made live take effect at a documented next safe decision boundary (e.g., price quote policy on a new quote, assignment on task completion), never retroactively.
- The game remains playable at normal speed in busy plants; observation, KPIs and decision prompts stay visible.

### 4.10 Business events, consultants and competition supply consequences

The reference combined product price/supply movement, rival factory indicators, paid company intelligence, R&D competition and external event reports. GS's account mentions news-video clips accompanying business shocks. Do not confuse the _presence of televised clips_ with proof that each clip was dynamically selected by a fully simulated economy.

For FreeIndustry, **events should be endogenous where possible**: a regional supply outage raises transit prices because actual shipments fail; a new competitor's capacity reduces prices because the market gains available offers; a major invention changes consumer willingness to buy; a cash shortage causes late payments because a bank balance is insufficient.

Research/competitive intelligence should inform decisions without reading omniscient secret truth unless the game mode explicitly allows it. A report can include `source`, `as_of`, `sample`, `confidence`, `estimate`, and `cost`.

## 6. Recommended FreeIndustry simulation contracts (new design, not recovered algorithms)

These are **implementable FreeIndustry invariants** motivated by the reference and the current project direction. They are intentionally stronger than any claim made about the 1996 software.

### 6.1 Authoritative object model

At minimum, keep durable entities for:

- `World`, `Region`, `City`, `IndustrialProperty`, `Lease`, `Firm`, `Facility`.
- `ProductDefinition`, `Recipe`, `MaterialDefinition`, `MaterialLot`, `InventoryLocation`, `StorageReservation`.
- `EquipmentDefinition`, `PlacedEquipment`, `MachinePort`, `MachineWorkstation`, `ConveyorSegment`, `MaterialHandlingDevice`, `MachineCycle`.
- `Person`, `SkillProfile`, `EmploymentAgreement`, `Shift`, `RoleAssignment`, `Task`, `TaskEvent`, `WorkstationReservation`.
- `Customer`, `Supplier`, `Quote`, `PurchaseOrder`, `SalesOrder`, `Delivery`, `Invoice`, `Payment`, `BankAccount`, `LedgerEntry`.
- `Technology`, `ResearchProject`, `Experiment`, `ResearchResult`, `CompetitorIntelligence`.
- `PolicyVersion`, `Alert`, `Incident`, `FacilityReport`, `MarketObservation`.

Avoid having separate independent copies of inventory in the rendering layer, warehouse model and finance model. One authoritative ledger for each quantity is the rule; summaries and sprites are projections.

### 6.2 Minimal physical-production state machine

A machine's own states should be explicit, e.g. `UNINSTALLED` → `IDLE` → `RESERVING_INPUTS` → `WAITING_FOR_OPERATOR` → `LOADING` → `PROCESSING` → `UNLOADING` → `READY_FOR_NEXT_JOB`. Alternate interruption states may be `STARVED`, `BLOCKED_OUTPUT`, `BROKEN`, `MAINTENANCE`, `DISABLED` or `UNPOWERED`.

1. A production job reserves input _lots_; reservations cannot exceed remaining unreserved goods.
2. Material handlers receive spatial delivery tasks and cannot deliver quantities that are not actually at their origin.
3. When production starts, required input is consumed **exactly once**, at the documented stage (start or completion; choose and test).
4. Completed outputs are placed in a free machine output buffer; if no space exists, the machine blocks without generating negative inventory.
5. Outputs transferred by workers or conveyor reduce their origin and increase destination with the same batch identity.
6. Machine downtime halts work and records a reason; repair requires permissible labor and possibly materials.
7. Successful dispatch removes goods from the warehouse and creates a documented sale/invoice event.
8. Each physical event appears as a coherent animation and timestamped event, not the other way around.

### 6.3 Labor and policy state machine

- Agent needs and shift attendance influence **when** a task is performed, but should not produce duplicate task completion.
- Exactly one location and one full-capacity physical task per person at any simulation instant.
- A policy change is versioned. New POs/quotes use the policy valid at their decision time; already-accepted contracts retain the terms agreed unless renegotiated.
- Idle staff can engage in alternative tasks, learning, recovery or amenities but retain a reason for their current activity.
- Reports compute actual time allocations (`working`, `travel`, `waiting`, `break`, `absent`, `other`) from the task timeline; duration totals reconcile with shifts.
- When a manager raises the wage, payroll and promised compensation immediately reflect the effective date, not arbitrary annualized deductions.
- Employment termination cancels or reassigns outstanding work at a safe boundary; it must not strand stock in a nonexistent person's inventory.

### 6.4 Market, customer and finance contracts

- A product sale has a real `buyer`, `seller`, `SKU`, `quantity`, `unit price`, `quality` and `terms`.
- A supplier cannot sell more available stock than it can reserve across concurrent orders, unless an explicit backorder mechanism exists.
- Each firm has finite cash/credit, production/stock and an expenditure budget, unless explicitly flagged as an infinite-liquidity sandbox counterparty.
- Purchase-order acceptance, physical delivery, invoice recognition and cash settlement are separate states; distinguish booked revenue from cash receipts.
- Every money movement passes a balanced posting with an audit trail; use integer minor currency units and defined rounding.
- Repeated processing, save/load, pause/resume and fast-forward cannot duplicate wages, sales, loan drawdowns, raw materials, finished goods or payments.
- Competitor reports derive from rival holdings/activity or acknowledged approximations, not an invented comparison number.

### 6.5 Time and determinism contracts

- A paused simulation executes **zero** simulation-time transitions.
- Rendering frame rate has no effect on firm finances, market quantities or machine output.
- Identical seed, input events, game version and time horizon produce identical authoritative state.
- Event priorities are stable; two events at the same timestamp have a deterministic tie-breaker.
- `Advance until <condition>` halts at the first eligible event boundary and does not skip a player-required decision.
- At high speed, skipped visual in-between animation never skips the underlying logistic or financial transition.
- Save/resume yields exactly the same future as uninterrupted execution given the same commands and seed.

### 6.6 Performance design for large factories

The 1996 game was criticized for slow animation and fixed turns. FreeIndustry should avoid making every market trade or agent movement wait on expensive graphical updates. Keep a **deterministic simulation kernel**, a separately throttled renderer, batched pathfinding, event-driven bookkeeping, sensible distant-facility level of detail and a typed audit log. A distant plant may aggregate some visuals but must preserve constraints on its people, machine-hours, inventory and shipping. Do not simulate competitors as limitless magic revenue generators for performance convenience.

### 7.1 Five indispensable cross-system acceptance stories

These stories link separate development stages so that a stage cannot claim success while the world remains inconsistent.

**A. First production run from nothing.** The starting firm has a leased industrial shell, limited funding and no owned equipment, stock or employee. Player buys and places equipment, posts ads, hires staff, buys supplies, and schedules work. Material arrives at a real dock; a handler moves it to a machine; the machine visibly processes it; finished goods appear physically and in inventory. Reports record actual spending and output. If **any** piece is implicit, the test fails.

**B. Shortage propagates properly.** One supplier unexpectedly stops shipping a key input. A purchasing agent notices, alternative prices rise if alternatives are scarce, on-hand stock buys time, production eventually waits at an identifiable station, salesperson cannot sell unpromised stock beyond available/committed quantities, and a management alert links to the shortage. No arbitrary global `demandPenalty` is applied as a substitute for this causal chain.

**C. A better factory layout saves money for a reason.** Two otherwise identical seeded facilities differ only by the distance and access between inbound stock, the machine and output storage. The shorter valid route improves observed handler utilization/throughput or reduces staff need. Both routes conserve the same material and charge real machine/labor costs; the benefit arises from fewer actions or less travel, not a hidden placement multiplier.

**D. Research has a real opportunity cost.** Production machine A can make current market goods, but assigning it to a plausible R&D experiment uses time/materials and removes some sellable output. Research unlocks a new commercial option, but only an actual customer market makes it profitable; failed hypothesis can still provide knowledge. Reports show R&D payroll, wasted trial inputs and later returns separately.

**E. A fast-forwarded company survives without hallucinated production.** The business runs at ordinary speed for a set simulated month and again at accelerated/headless speed using same seed/commands. Finances, total wages, labor time, deliveries, machine cycles, maintenance and stock snapshots match exactly. Rendering may differ, but economic reality does not.

### 8.1 Protocol to upgrade an item to `VERIFIED_ORIGINAL`

A researcher must record the following as a single reproducible evidence packet:

1. **Game provenance:** exact game title and release, version/patch (`1.1103` if applicable), OS/VM conditions, whether demo/full game, save identification, reproducibility caveats.
2. **Question:** one precise falsifiable proposition (e.g., `Machine X does not produce when operator Y is absent`). Avoid compound claims.
3. **Starting state:** city, facility, date/time, workers/assignments, machine orientation, material stocks, bank balances and applicable realism modes.
4. **Actions:** minimal numbered sequence of user interactions with screenshots or self-recorded observations, without copying copyrighted source assets into the FreeIndustry repository.
5. **Observations:** raw game-time markers, stock/finance readouts and visible transitions; distinguish direct observation from inference.
6. **Replication:** at least two identical-condition runs where practical and one counterfactual that changes only one relevant input. If behavior seems random, provide sample counts instead of certainty.
7. **Verdict:** `confirmed` / `not reproducible` / `ambiguous` with known version, confidence scope and contradictions.
8. **Ledger patch:** link evidence packet, update original-behavior claim, confidence and status only to the extent the test justifies it. Leave algorithms `OPEN_QUESTION` unless independently established.

**Proposed evidence-packet naming:** `research/original-observations/OR-xx/YYYY-MM-DD_<topic>.md` with textual observations and _links_ to personal captures where lawful. Evidence packets are historical analysis, not game assets or source files.

### 8.2 Research priorities that should not block the development plan

- **Do not wait** for proof of original formulas before implementing FreeIndustry's own demand, payroll, needs or stock simulation.
- **Do not wait** for original art catalog identification before authoring new machinery and visual style.
- **Do not reproduce** rigid turns, fragile asset caps or overflow defects just to be closer to the original.
- **Do wait** for independent FreeIndustry acceptance tests before declaring that an observed worker movement equals real operational throughput.
- **Do keep** historical ambiguity in the ledger and mark modern replacements `EXTENDED` or original-inspired `RECONSTRUCTED` as appropriate.

## 9. Integration rules for `plan.md`, the specification and coding agents

1. **Never let this ledger silently rewrite the game design.** The user's FreeIndustry requirements and active specification control intended behavior; the historical ledger informs them. If they conflict, explicitly identify the divergence rather than selecting the historical limitation by default.
2. **Keep OR IDs stable.** Existing tests, commits and prompts may refer to OR-01 through OR-17. New research may add `OR-86`, etc., or supporting sub-records (`OR-38.A`) but must not repurpose IDs.
3. **Keep original and new rules in separate namespaces.** Historical descriptions are documentation; implementation source must reference FreeIndustry's own design constants and models. Historical dollar amounts or thresholds are not production balancing inputs.
4. **Stage integration:**
   - Steps **03, 06–09:** deterministic time, saves, dense UI, business setup, floor editor and views.
   - Steps **11–12:** people, shifts, tasks, personality/behavior and staffing histories.
   - Steps **14–16:** material ledger, physical handling, machines, customers/suppliers and dispatch.
   - Steps **18, 20–23:** reports, market and rival economy, facility expansion, delegation and negotiated commercial transactions.
   - Steps **24–26:** R&D, credit and competitor behavior.
   - Step **30:** high-level reports, challenge goals, comparison/ranking and coherence checks.
   - **Always:** save/replay determinism, spatial/financial conservation, and independent intellectual property.
5. **Source reporting format for feature work:** `Ledger IDs`, `Known original interaction`, `Unknown original mechanism`, `Selected FreeIndustry behavior`, `Tests`, `IP originality check`.
6. **Changed fidelity claims require evidence.** A developer agent must not change `RECONSTRUCTED` to `VERIFIED_ORIGINAL` because it built something that behaves plausibly.
7. **No balance by anecdote.** Walkthrough advice (particular suggested wages, sizes, early products, office choices or capacity rules) may motivate test cases, but should not be treated as hidden ground truth.
8. **Prefer observable causal systems.** If a metric improves, be able to explain the actual task/transaction/inventory mechanism that caused it. Avoid scoring bonuses with no operating-world counterpart unless explicitly communicated as a difficulty rule.
9. **Do not copy** original software, scripts, UI art, audio, video, textual descriptions, sprite geometry or substantial game-data compilations. Functional concepts may inspire a separately authored implementation. Keep attribution truthful: “inspired by the gameplay of _Free Enterprise_ (1996, Tsunami Media)” where suitable in project credits, not in in-game branding that suggests endorsement.
10. **Review periodically:** new primary evidence can narrow claims but must never overwrite the audit trail. Record date, source, changed statement and affected tests.

### Bottom line for design and code reviews

**Preserve the business fantasy:** build the firm, physically place machines, hire fallible people, buy parts from suppliers, see goods go through believable manufacturing stages, make deals, watch the finances, confront capacity limits, innovate and compete. **Do not inherit the documented pain points:** uninterruptible turns, opaque algorithms, obsolete graphical assets, arbitrary caps, hard-to-use tools and misleading reports. **Never claim original-formula fidelity where the evidence covers only an observable interface or reported outcome.**
